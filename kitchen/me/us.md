That’s us, on the right, me and ammi (امی). Despite what my face conveys, I'm not having a good time. Unfortunately, this informal baptism into the culinary life didn't really kick in until about 2 decades later.

Like a lot of people I knew, and read about online, I picked up cooking some months into the pandemic. I was living in Montreal at the time, and so before the pandemic, I made regular visits back home to Toronto. And as any South Asian can attest to, I always went back with a lot of food. Ammi's goal was literally to keep me stocked up until the next time I visit. So, given that I had a freezer that ammi insists she had a claim on, I didn't really need to do much cooking.

So, when the pandemic began, those infrequent visits came to an abrupt stop. And unknowningly I went cold turkey on ammi's food. And then the withdrawal began. I didn't cook much at the time but my partner did. My non-desi partner. So, when I asked her to cook some food "like my mom does", you can imagine it didn't go well -- for either of us. So, one day I just decided that I'm going to take matters into my own hands. 

I started by video calling ammi. She was going to walk me through some recipes. Truthfully, I can't recall what the first recipe was, but I imagine it must have been chicken ki tarkari, since that has always been a comfort food for me.

At the culmination of the first recipe, I recall tasting it right at the end (because obviously it didn't make sense to taste it throughout). It tasted off. I described the taste to my mom. And then she immediately said "show me the teaspoon you used to add the zeera". So, I took out the bundled sets of measuring spoons and showed her the teaspoon measure. She facepalms, and says "That's not a teaspoon! I mean the spoon you use to stir the tea!". Yeah, of course, why wouldn't I think that? Well, I guess it kind of makes sense? Anyway. I added more zeera, and that kind of helped, but the damage had been done. Cooking, largely, is a one-way (irreversible) process. 

Anyone who grew up with immigrant parents probably knows there's always those little things that don't quite match up between your understanding and your parents' understanding. However, there was something that my mom and I have always been in sync about, and it came up even in the little anecdote I mention above. How did my mom know it was zeera? There are a handful of other ingredients, but my mom knew that the zeera must have been off. Well, it wasn't obvious to me why, but it kind of goes back to my childhood.
